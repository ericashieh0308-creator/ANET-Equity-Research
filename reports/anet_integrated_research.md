# Business, demand and competition
### Revenue model and customer groups
Reported: FY2025 product revenue was 7,576.9 and service revenue was 1,428.8. Management describes products as switching/routing systems and associated network applications, while services primarily reflect post-contract support and renewals. Service revenue is not automatically standalone software subscriptions or annual recurring revenue. [K, Item 7 “Revenue” p.54; Item 8 p.69]

Management identifies large cloud/AI operators, internet/service providers, AI neoclouds, enterprises and government customers. Distribution includes direct sales and distributors, resellers, systems integrators and OEM partners. These are issuer descriptions, not independent estimates of customer economics. [K, Item 1 “Our Customers” / “Sales and Marketing” p.7]

### Products and the role of software
Management describes EOS as its network operating system, CloudVision as its management/automation platform and NetDL as its network data lake. Its portfolio includes data-center/AI Ethernet switching, campus networking, routing and support; Etherlink addresses AI networking use cases. A common software and management architecture is an asserted differentiator; independent customer evidence is needed to establish superior operating economics. [K, Item 1 pp.1–4,6]

### AI networking: a demand thesis, not a revenue attribution
Management links demand to AI/cloud workloads and discusses scale-up, scale-out and scale-across architectures. AI infrastructure can require networking capacity, but deployment architecture, acceptance conditions and customer budgets govern which vendors capture revenue. Management also cautions that AI investment can change other spending priorities. No AI-specific revenue amount, market size or market-share estimate is asserted. [K, Item 1 pp.2–3; Q, Item 2 “Overview” pp.14–15]

### Analyst interpretation and evidence required
Ethernet adoption alone does not establish an Arista win. Evaluate like-for-like scale-up, scale-out and inter-site requirements, customer procurement constraints and implementation costs. The report does not rank price/performance, claim technology superiority or assign a valuation premium for AI exposure. Independent deployment economics, renewal data and dated customer-spending evidence remain research gaps. [R3, Sections 2–3 and evidence gaps]

Large-customer spending and acceptance cycles can create volatility. Monitor diversification and accepted deployments rather than treating AI demand as assured revenue. [K, Item 1 p.7; Item 1A pp.14–15]

# Forecast construction and FCFF methodology
### Base annual forecast: HYPOTHETICAL / NOT YET VALIDATED
FY2026 is a partial-year estimate: 9M income, D&A and PP&E purchases are reported; Q4 is modeled. Annual operating taxes, intangible investment and economic NWC are modeled. FY2027–FY2030 are entirely estimates. Values below are rounded for presentation, not independently entered. [R1; model_outputs/forecasts.csv]

{{forecast}}

### Explicit forecast mechanics
FY2026 revenue = reported 9M2026 + derived Q4 FY2025 revenue × (1 + assumed Q4 growth). Q4 FY2025 revenue of 2,487.8 is FY2025 less 9M2025, not a separately reported line. Later years use scenario growth. EBIT (operating income) = gross profit − OpEx; NOPAT (after-tax operating profit) = EBIT − modeled taxes. [K p.69; Q p.2; R1]

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + D&A − CapEx − change in operating NWC. No interest expense or dividend is subtracted. No SBC add-back or arbitrary repurchase outflow is added: compensation remains an operating cost. Existing awards still require a separate dilution/claim assessment to avoid double charging future service costs. [R1–R2]

### Timing and classification audit
Only Q4 FY2026 FCFF enters the DCF at 0.25 years; FY2027–FY2030 use 1.25–4.25 years. Full-year FY2026 FCFF is not discounted in addition. Annual modeled FCFF less elapsed-9M modeled FCFF reconciles to the Q4 stub in all three cases. Elapsed-9M normalized taxes/intangibles/NWC are modeled, not reported FCFF. [R2; stub_audit.csv]

The current-only NWC proxy rises from 267.5 at December 2025 to 1,051.3 at September 2026. Including noncurrent deferred revenue changes the increase from 783.8 to 416.3. Neither is economically validated; reclassification requires recalibrated targets, not a one-time cash release. [Q p.1; R2; nwc_classifications.csv]

Annual combined purchases lack a reliable split. The model adds a hypothetical 0.5%-of-revenue intangible budget; a separate PP&E-only alternative omits it to isolate its effect. That alternative is incomplete if intangibles are needed, not evidence of zero actual spending or a validated upper bound. [R2; capex_alternatives.csv]

# Scenario enterprise valuation and sensitivity
HYPOTHETICAL / NOT YET VALIDATED • USD millions • September 30, 2026 retrospective reference

{{valuation}}

Enterprise value = sum of discounted forecast FCFF + discounted terminal value. Terminal value = next-year terminal FCFF / (WACC − terminal growth); WACC must exceed terminal growth. Terminal FCFF recomputes NOPAT, D&A, CapEx and NWC investment rather than merely growing the last FCFF. These are operating-asset values, not market capitalizations or equity valuations. [R1; scenario_dcf.csv]

### Base operating case: WACC versus terminal growth
{{sensitivity}}

Sensitivity values are enterprise values in USD millions. Only WACC and terminal growth change within this Base grid; operating assumptions remain fixed. Cross-scenario differences above also change growth, margins and reinvestment. The grid is not a probability distribution, price target range or market-implied valuation. [R1; sensitivity.csv]

### Terminal economics and valuation dependence
{{terminal}}

The implied incremental return equals terminal growth / (net reinvestment / NOPAT), with net reinvestment = CapEx − D&A + change in NWC. It is not reported accounting ROIC. These high implied returns require validation; terminal growth and reinvestment are not independent free inputs. Terminal value represents roughly 69–84% of modeled EV across the cases, making mature economics particularly important. [R2; terminal_economics.csv; scenario_dcf.csv]

Original scenario EVs are unchanged. WACC and terminal economics still require evidence. Separate CapEx-scope alternatives isolate a modeling choice rather than establish a recommended valuation range. [R2; U]
